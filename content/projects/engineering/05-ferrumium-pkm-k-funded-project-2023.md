Problem:

Thousands of used cigarette lighters are discarded every day despite containing reusable components and distinctive mechanical forms. The challenge was to transform this waste into a product that was both environmentally meaningful and commercially attractive.

Approach / Process:

Contributed to the design and development of Ferrumium, a collection of miniature robot figures created from recycled lighter components. Developed product concepts, explored design variations, and assisted in transforming raw materials into visually appealing collectibles. Worked closely with the team to balance aesthetics, manufacturability, and material constraints while maintaining the project's sustainability objectives.

Outcome:

Contributed to the successful development of an innovative upcycling product that secured funding through the PKM-K entrepreneurship program. The project demonstrated how discarded materials could be transformed into value-added products with creative and commercial potential.

Key Learnings:

- Sustainable product design and upcycling principles.

- Designing under material and manufacturing constraints.

- Converting conceptual ideas into tangible products.

- Cross-functional collaboration in an entrepreneurship project.
